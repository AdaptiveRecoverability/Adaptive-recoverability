What ARG Watches

Institutions can remain solvent, operational, and outwardly stable — while losing the capacity to recover.

This is the zombie zone: a regime where a system continues to function, but can no longer correct itself without external support. ARG measures depth, velocity, and acceleration into this zone — the leading indicators no conventional framework tracks.

The Problem

Conventional metrics measure stability, not capacity to recover.

VaR, stress testing, and catastrophe models all assume the institution being measured retains the ability to self-correct. That assumption is rarely tested — and rarely true at the moment it matters most.

Why It Matters

Institutions often look healthy immediately before they fail.

Japanese banks in the 1990s, Lehman Brothers in 2008, Silicon Valley Bank in 2023 — each passed contemporaneous solvency checks before structural correction capacity had already been lost.

The Evidence

Applied retrospectively, the pattern is consistent.

The same structural pattern is identifiable in every case, well ahead of the visible event — applied retrospectively to known historical failures.
